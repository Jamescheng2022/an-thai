Why CSL Sonic Logging Shows False Defects in Bangkok Bored Piles: The Acoustic Tube Debonding Trap

How thermal contraction causes CSL tube debonding anomalies in Bangkok bored piles, and how Thai engineers distinguish debonding from genuine concrete defects.

Why Tube Debonding Mimics Concrete Defects

Crosshole Sonic Logging (CSL) measures ultrasonic arrival time and energy attenuation between water-filled access tubes cast inside bored piles. When a PVC or steel access tube separates slightly from the surrounding concrete during curing shrinkage or thermal contraction, the microscopic air gap delays or attenuates the ultrasonic wave — producing a trace that superficially resembles honeycombing or necking.

Distinguishing Debonding From Structural Anomalies

Field engineers compare all perimeter and diagonal tube pairs at the same elevation. True cross-sectional concrete defects typically affect multiple diagonal paths across the core, whereas localized tube debonding appears primarily on profiles involving a single tube, often in the upper shaft or above groundwater.

Practical Verification Steps in Thailand

Before authorizing costly coring or remediation, verify tube material (steel vs. PVC), curing age at test time, water-filling level, and repeatability across all tube combinations. Any engineering disposition remains governed by the project specification and responsible licensed Thai engineer.
