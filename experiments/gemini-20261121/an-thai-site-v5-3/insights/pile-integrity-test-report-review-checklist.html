When a Pile Integrity Report Says “Pass” but the Waveform Looks Odd: Seven Questions

Seven practical questions to assess a low-strain pile integrity report, trace source records, and choose an evidence-led next step.

Why a “Pass” Label Is Not the Whole Answer

A low-strain impact test helps screen a pile for possible integrity-related changes, but interpretation depends on pile material, geometry, accessibility, site conditions, and signal quality. Low-strain response does not directly establish bearing capacity or load-settlement behavior.

Seven Questions for Technical Review

1) Is this the right engineering question (integrity screening vs. bearing capacity)? 2) Can the signal be matched to the exact pile ID, as-built drawing, and test date? 3) Does the report document pile-head preparation, sensor coupling, and site constraints? 4) Are representative raw traces reviewable with readable axes and wave-speed assumptions? 5) Were pile geometry, concreting/driving history, and ground layers considered? 6) Does the conclusion separate observed signal features from uncertainty? 7) Which contractually adopted method and acceptance criteria govern the decision?

A Practical Handover Request

When clarifying an unfamiliar waveform, share the pile schedule, accurate pile ID, construction/ground records, and the original test report. Request written reconciliation of the evidence, assumptions, and proposed next step rather than an unsupported label change.
