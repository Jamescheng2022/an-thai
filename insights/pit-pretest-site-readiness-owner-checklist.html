Before Booking a Pile Integrity Test in Thailand: The Owner's Site-Readiness Pack

By AN-THAI Engineering · 2026-11-09

Why an apparently simple PIT booking can go wrong

A project manager may ask for a pile integrity test as soon as the concrete is in place. But the useful question is not simply whether someone can bring a hammer and sensor to site. It is whether the selected piles, installation records, access conditions and intended engineering decision have been agreed before the crew arrives.

Low-strain pile integrity testing (PIT) records the response to a light impact and may help investigate continuity or significant changes along suitable deep foundations. It is not a bearing-capacity test. This guide is for owners, main contractors and site QA teams organizing concrete-pile integrity testing; it is not a universal procedure for steel sections, very short piles, heavily capped piles or every foundation geometry.

Build a usable pile list, not merely a total quantity

Send a marked plan and a pile register with stable pile IDs, locations, pile type, nominal section, design or constructed length if known, installation date, and the reason for each selected test. Provide the latest drawings as well as any changes between design and construction. A request for '200 piles' without a traceable register invites testing the wrong element or producing results that cannot be linked confidently to the permanent works.

Installation history matters. Share relevant driving or concreting records, interruptions, suspected obstructions, unexpected spoil or volume changes, repairs and previous tests. Add the borehole or ground-investigation information available for the test area. The equipment maker specifically recommends considering installation records and soil profiles when interpreting PIT reflections. A missing record should be labeled missing rather than reconstructed from memory.

Prepare the actual pile head and a safe work window

Confirm which pile heads are exposed and accessible, whether site housekeeping allows an inspection, and who will escort the testing team. For ordinary top-impact PIT, there must be a suitable concrete surface for the impact and motion-sensing device. The testing engineer should decide the impact arrangement and whether special access, preparation or an alternative method is necessary. Do not instruct the contractor to cut, notch or alter a structural element from a generic web diagram.

Coordinate concrete maturity or curing restrictions with the project specification and engineer; do not assume every installation can be tested the next morning. Arrange safe access, sufficient working room, a reliable identification mark and a practical sequence that avoids clashing with ongoing pile breaking, excavation, concreting or lifting. Work-at-height, lifting and temporary works questions remain the contractor's and competent professionals' responsibilities.

Set the evidence question before discussing a pass or fail

Ask the designated design or geotechnical engineer what the PIT data are intended to establish and what will happen if a trace is unclear. Distinguish a routine integrity screening exercise from an investigation of a known construction anomaly. Specify who will assess the records, which additional records they may consult, and when a follow-up investigation or an alternative method could be warranted.

The historic ASTM D5882-16 method was withdrawn in 2025 and ASTM currently lists no replacement. If project documents still reference that edition, the engineer and authority or employer must confirm the controlling contractual and approval basis; do not advertise a withdrawn document as a currently active standard. Manufacturer descriptions support understanding of the instrument, not automatic acceptance criteria for a Thai project. A clean-looking trace is not itself a design sign-off.

Agree a four-party handover and an exception path

Before mobilization, let the owner or QA representative define the decision sought; let the contractor confirm access and the matching as-built records; let the testing specialist confirm the feasible acquisition arrangement; and let the responsible engineer identify the review and escalation path. The parties' contractual duties may differ, but these four questions still need an answer.

Specify how test IDs will be cross-checked against the site register, who will attend to resolve doubtful labels, how data and photographs may be shared, and what happens if a pile head cannot be tested safely. For unusual reflections, request the raw signal, notes on the testing conditions and comparison with neighbouring piles before making an irreversible remedial decision. Keep test results, observations and professional interpretations separate.

What to include when requesting a scoped quotation

A useful first enquiry contains the project location and phase, the proposed pile schedule, an annotated site plan, expected test quantity and timing window, pile type and dimensions, available installation and geotechnical records, a description of pile-head access, and the specific question the testing is expected to inform. Remove confidential customer identifiers if sharing is not authorized.

Frequently asked questions

Can PIT prove that a pile can carry its design load?

No. Low-strain integrity information is not a direct axial bearing-capacity measurement. Capacity questions need an engineer-selected method and project-specific evidence.

What if some installation records are missing?

Submit the available pile register, drawings and ground data and list the missing records explicitly. The engineer can assess the limitations and whether additional investigation is needed.

Is an ASTM reference automatically mandatory in Thailand?

No. The governing edition and applicability depend on project specifications, contracts and competent authorities or engineers. D5882-16 is withdrawn; do not present it as an active standard.
